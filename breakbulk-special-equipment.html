Choosing the right method

Open top, flat rack or breakbulk?

Out-of-gauge cargo is defined by its size or weight, while breakbulk describes how it is shipped. The right choice depends on your cargo’s shape, weight and route.

Over-height

Open-top containers

For tall cargo that cannot pass through a closed container door. The load is craned in from above and covered with a tarpaulin.

  • Loaded from above by crane
  • Over-height planned in advance
  • Weather cover and lashing
Over-width & heavy

Flat-rack containers

An open platform with end walls for wide, heavy or awkward loads that can overhang within permitted limits, secured with engineered lashing.

  • Handles wide and heavy loads
  • Pre-lashed for easier terminal handling
  • Overhang agreed with the carrier
Too big for a box

Breakbulk

For pieces too large or heavy to containerise. Cargo is lifted by crane, set on bedding and lashed onto several flat racks or directly on the vessel.

  • Lifting and stowage plan
  • Crane and stevedore handling
  • Bedding and engineered securing

Good to know

Breakbulk & equipment questions answered

The basics of shipping heavy and out-of-gauge cargo. Can’t find your answer? Talk to our team.

Still have a question?+91 98982 86205

Breakbulk is cargo that is shipped outside a standard container – typically heavy or oversized items that are lifted by crane and lashed on flat racks or directly on the vessel. It is a handling method, so a piece can be breakbulk even if it is not huge.

Out-of-gauge cargo exceeds the standard dimensions or weight limits for transport. It can still travel containerised on open-top or flat-rack equipment, or as breakbulk when it is too large for any container.

Open-top containers suit tall cargo loaded from above, while flat racks suit wide or heavy loads that can overhang within permitted limits. Use the cargo fit checker above for a quick indication, and our team confirms the final choice.

Length, width, height and weight of each piece, photos or drawings, centre of gravity if known, the port of loading and the final destination, and your ready date. The more detail we have, the more accurate the plan.

We check road limits, bridge clearances and any permits required for inland moves, and customs brokerage (CHB) is part of our Freight Forwarding Division, so clearance can be coordinated with the shipment.
